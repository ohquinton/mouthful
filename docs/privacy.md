# SaidClearly privacy policy

Last updated: 28 October 2026

SaidClearly helps you practise what you'll say out loud. This page explains what happens to your voice, your words and your data. The short version: your practice stays on your phone.

## Your voice

- **SaidClearly listens only while you practise**: when a topic talk, reading or word drill is running.
- **Speech to text.** Your phone's speech recognition turns what you say into text so SaidClearly can follow along.
  - When your phone can do this itself for the English you picked, your voice is not sent anywhere.
  - When it can't, SaidClearly asks you first. If you agree, your audio goes to your phone's speech service while you practise: Apple on iPhone, or your phone's speech service (often Google) on Android. Their own privacy policies apply. You can withdraw this agreement at any time in **You → Privacy**.
- **Recordings** are kept only if you switch them on (they start off). They are saved only on your phone, in SaidClearly's own folder. They are not uploaded by SaidClearly. Like other app files, they may be included in your phone's own iCloud or Google backup if you use one.
- **Tester mode** (supervised beta only, off for everyone else) records every reading session and keeps what the phone heard, on the phone, so a tester and the developer can review it together.

## What stays on your phone

Your sessions, scores, scripts, word lists, settings and recordings are stored on your phone. SaidClearly has no account and no server that holds them.

## Usage counts (analytics)

SaidClearly can send anonymous usage counts to PostHog (USA) to see which parts of the app help, for example "session finished", a score, a duration or a word count. Each phone gets a random ID that isn't linked to your name, email or Apple ID.

- We **never** send your words, scripts, transcripts or voice. The app removes any text before sending.
- We **never** send the English variety you picked, since it could hint at where you're from.
- PostHog can see the internet address a request comes from. We don't use it to identify you.
- You can switch usage counts off in **You → Privacy** (the same switch turns off crash reports), and they stay off if you reset the app.

## Crash reports

If SaidClearly crashes, it can send a crash report to Sentry (USA) so we can fix the bug. A crash report contains the type of error, a short error message, where in the app's code it happened, the app version, and your phone's model and operating system version.

- We **never** send your words, scripts, transcripts or voice. Any report whose error message looks like ordinary text (more than a few words) is thrown away on your phone before sending, and error messages are cut short.
- Crash reports don't include screenshots, what was on screen, what you did before the crash, your language or region settings, your name, or any account. Sentry is set not to store the internet address a report comes from.
- Crash reports use the same switch as usage counts: **You → Privacy → Share anonymous usage counts and crash reports**. Turn it off and nothing more is sent (it takes full effect the next time you open the app). It stays off if you reset the app.

## Purchases

Subscriptions are handled by Apple and RevenueCat. SaidClearly never sees your payment details. RevenueCat receives a random app user ID and your purchase status so your Pro access works. See RevenueCat's privacy policy.

## Deleting your data

- **You → Delete recordings** deletes every recording file on the phone.
- **You → Reset all data** deletes your sessions, scripts, words, settings and recordings.
- Deleting the app deletes everything SaidClearly stored on the phone.
- Usage counts already sent can't be tied back to you, but you can ask us to delete them for your phone's ID: a.twist100@gmail.com.

## Children

SaidClearly is not made for children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect anything from them.

## Changes and contact
